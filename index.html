Notes, minus the part where you stop to take them.

The moment worth keeping and the moment you have time to write it down are never the same one. So Casrion puts the whole detour on a single key. You copy the line, you press it, you keep reading.

Like that. Select any sentence here and click the tag.

In the app that key is Ctrl+Shift+C. Your browser keeps that one for its dev tools and will not hand it over, so on this page you click instead. The headings and undo below are real.

The key decides the shape

A capture that lands as a wall of text is barely better than the clipboard. So the key you press picks how it arrives: heading, code, bold, plain. It comes out as Markdown, in a file you chose, on a disk you own.

  • All of them Ctrl+Shift+H

Showing Mac keys. A few are not a straight Ctrl to Cmd swap, because macOS keeps those combinations for itself.

It remembers where you got it

A quote with no source is half a note. Every capture can carry the page it came from, the app, and the time. They are stacking up in the notebook right now. Untick the box and watch them go.

And it explains what you don't know

Sometimes the problem is not keeping the sentence, it is that the sentence means nothing to you yet. Select it, tap Ctrl twice, and a small window explains it where you are standing. It reads the window around your selection, so it knows a stray symbol belongs to the formula three lines up.

That is a language model on your own disk, so nothing is sent anywhere. It stays unloaded until you look like you need it and hands the memory back five minutes after you stop. Idle, the whole app sits under 450 MB.

Thoughts that started in your head

Not everything is copied. A small writer floats over whatever you are doing, takes the thought, and vanishes, with Markdown working as you type. Or hold a key and just say it, for the thoughts that typing would kill.

Nothing leaves your machine

Casrion makes no network requests. Not fewer than most, none. Delete it tomorrow and every note you made is still sitting there, readable in Notepad.

Mac wants two minutes first

Apple charges for a signature too, so macOS treats Casrion as a stranger once. Then it is done forever.

  1. Drag it into Applications

    Straight out of the .dmg.

  2. Right click it, choose Open

    Then Open again in the dialog. That gets you past the first launch block. Still refusing? Run xattr -cr /Applications/Casrion.app in Terminal once, then open it normally.

  3. Switch on Accessibility

    System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility. Explain needs it to read what you highlighted. Casrion opens that pane for you the first time you ask it to explain something.

  4. Switch on Input Monitoring, if you want the double tap

    macOS guards raw key taps separately. Skip this and Cmd+Shift+E still works fine.

  5. Quit and reopen once

    From the menu bar icon, so macOS actually applies the switches. The microphone and browser questions just appear when you first need them, click Allow.

Fair questions

Windows says it protected my PC.
SmartScreen, for any installer without an expensive certificate. Nothing was found. More info, then Run anyway.
Where do my notes live?
Markdown files in folders you pick, with screenshots and voice memos in an assets folder beside them. They are just files.
Does it need internet?
Never. Explain downloads its model once if you turn it on, and after that even that works on a plane.
How do I quit it, or update it?
Closing the window leaves it capturing from the tray, which is the point. Right click the tray icon to really quit. To update, run the new installer over the old one. Your notes are untouched.
My Mac says Casrion cannot be opened.
Right click it in Applications and choose Open, then Open again. If it still refuses, xattr -cr /Applications/Casrion.app in Terminal once.
I updated on Mac and a permission stopped working.
macOS ties them to the exact copy, so the checkmarks can go stale while still looking on. Remove Casrion from Accessibility and Input Monitoring with −, add it back with +, quit and reopen.
